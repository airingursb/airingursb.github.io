NO. 01Reply

Airing to Qinghe

22 Feb 2026

Hello, Qinghe,

Reading your letter feels like meeting you. Thank you for following my work and for your trust. I can sense your anxiety: [age removed] and [length of career break removed] are indeed a heavy combination.

Here are a few thoughts on your two questions, for you to consider.

On the first question: I think product management has considerable potential in the AI era. Many engineers lack product sense, whereas this is a PM’s specialty. As AI lowers technical barriers, knowing how to make a good product matters even more. I would not change fields blindly. Instead, look for enterprise product or solutions roles that make use of your presales experience, and first bring your career break to an end.

I also know too little about [the reader’s field] to offer a suitable answer about postgraduate study. You need to decide in light of your ambitions and what you love. If it serves neither, and is simply a way to avoid today’s employment difficulties, I would not recommend it.

On the second question:

The drive to keep learning: my approach is to make myself produce something, whether an article or a small project. Making something of my own gives me more motivation to continue learning. I enjoy the process of creating, and without continued learning I cannot satisfy that desire to create. Inner drive is not something you “find”; it is something you build by doing.

Developing a habit of thinking deeply: perhaps it starts with not taking decisions for granted, and asking about the reasons behind things. Sometimes you uncover a different perspective, and that discovery brings a sense of accomplishment. Do not always rely on System 1 to decide; deliberately cultivate your System 2. Conversations with AI can also help you practice deeper thinking.

Happy New Year. I hope you soon find your way through the uncertainty and discover a rhythm of your own.

Airing
