NO. 12Letter

Shiqing to Airing

7 Jun 2024

Airing, hello.

I recently started reading blogs, and followed links from other people's sites until I found yours. I have read all your annual reviews and some of the monthly posts. I am in a period of confusion and uncertainty, and hope to hear your thoughts and advice. I really appreciate the insight and wisdom in your writing, so I am taking the liberty of writing in the hope that you might offer some inspiration and help.

Let me briefly introduce myself: I am a university student, 〔year of study, region, university category and specific subject withheld〕. I am a textbook example of a small-town student trained to do well in exams. Throughout 〔specific length of schooling withheld〕, all I have known is solving exam questions. I know nothing about what lies beyond them: living, making friends, dreaming. And because I now spend all my time outside class playing games, even my ability to answer exam questions is slipping away. With graduation season approaching, I have started thinking about what I might do after graduating. Because I dislike school and lack confidence in my academic ability, my current plan is to find work after my bachelor's degree. Many of my past choices have been made this way: eliminating what I dislike most, which means I do not necessarily like the option left over. Making a decision by elimination is easy for me, but I have found that this way of choosing makes me increasingly dislike my own life.

Reading many people's blogs recently has convinced me of the value of producing something, so I want to start writing regularly too. But when I open a document to write, I find that I do not care about my choices, my life, or even my thoughts. I do not know when this began, but I am aware of it now. I prepared for the university entrance exam because that was what my surroundings demanded. I chose this university and subject because I imagined they would make me money, and at university I have been content just to pass. Yet when I look back at all I have done, then and now, I have no thoughts about any of it. I do not know how to describe it: it does not matter; it is all the same. This state now leaves me unable to take an interest in anything. I have tried learning an instrument, drawing, traveling, and buying a Switch to play games I had wanted for a long time. But even when I achieve or acquire something, I feel no emotional movement. I no longer feel happy.

This is not the first time I have experienced this. In the past, however, I treated Rilke's words—

“I want to ask you, as best I can, to be patient with everything unresolved in your heart, and to love the questions themselves, like locked rooms or books written in an unfamiliar language. Do not seek answers now that cannot yet be given to you, because you could not yet live them. Everything must be lived. For now, live in the questions.”

—as a guiding principle, and so I have continued living in this confusion and indifference. But I now find that staying in this fog for so long has made it impossible for me to take another step, because I keep telling myself that everywhere in the fog is the same. I am not afraid of being inadequate. I am afraid that I may no longer be able to improve. Yet I need to improve now if I am to achieve my goals.

I originally intended to ask for advice about study and work, but the more I write, the more I realize that I do not care about my choices, cannot feel my emotions, and prevent myself from making an effort unless it is absolutely necessary. This leaves me distressed and confused.

I am not in a good state. If my negative, dispirited words have made you uncomfortable, I sincerely apologize. I hope for your understanding and guidance.

7 Jun 2024

NO. 12Reply

Airing to Shiqing

16 Jun 2024

Hello.

I was also a textbook small-town exam student, born in a small, little-known city called 〔city withheld〕. Studying was the only way out. The education I received from childhood was therefore fairly oppressive. Perhaps the only happy memories were the good friends I made in middle school, followed by the dark years of high school. I attended 〔school and class withheld〕, where the competition was intense. The slightest relaxation would mean a lower score in the next monthly exam and even greater pressure afterward.

Looking back, I did not know what I liked then. I just mechanically answered questions and studied—if that could even be called studying. I did not know where the road ahead led. I simply followed, without feeling, the sequence of exams the adults had laid out for me. I did not even dare have ideas of my own, or develop what you might call dislike.

Unfortunately, I underperformed in the university entrance exam. It felt as though fate had played a cruel joke on me: 〔specific exam rankings withheld〕. The only failing grade I had ever received was in the mathematics paper of that exam. Very reluctantly, I settled for a university outside the major national university schemes.

But I was also fortunate. A lighter academic workload and a chance encounter helped me discover what I loved. I then spent all four university years moving toward the same goal, with relatively good results. I do not believe effort can change everything. My own experience had its share of dramatic turns, and one advantage I had over many people was discovering what I loved shortly after enrolling—something many may never reach in a lifetime. Whatever happens, though, do not reject your own life. Keep believing that what you love is still hidden somewhere in life, waiting for you.

I later reflected on why I was good at recognizing what I liked: cultivate sensitivity, try more things, and record more. In a monthly post about keeping a diary, I discussed its benefits. More specifically, I meant an emotional diary: before bed each day, attend to the changes in your feelings, try to record the happy things that happened, uncover those feelings, and correct emotional biases, nudging your emotional state in a more positive direction. This can be simple, and you can give it a try. A diary is for yourself. It needs no elaborate language or structure; a few keywords can be enough.

I suggest trying this for a month and seeing what happens. I hope my experience and suggestions can offer you some practical help.

I hope brighter feelings find you. I look forward to hearing from you.

16 Jun 2024
