NO. 19Letter

Qingyu to Airing

26 Jul 2023

Dear blogger, I hope this finds you well.

It is midsummer now. A full spring has passed since we last spoke, and my internship at [company removed] is almost over. I plan to return to university to prepare for the civil service examinations. My energy is limited, so I probably will not be able to take part in the autumn recruitment season.

At a departmental dinner today, I heard the usual conversations: “getting local residency, buying a home, preparing for the roughly equal split between academic and vocational education that the next generation faces.” I suddenly felt a sense of fate repeating itself—a group of high-achieving students from small towns passing the same cycle on to their children. What I most wish is that children need not be the whole of a person’s life, and that parents can have lives of their own. I really hope all those parents who work tirelessly for their children can also cultivate rich, fresh lives for themselves.

You may not know much about the points-based residency policy in [city removed]. In short, the threshold rises every year. These days, parents hoping to accumulate enough points generally have to wait until their children are nearly in secondary school. To secure the right for their children to sit the university entrance exam in [city removed], that wait is already approaching the limits of both the policy and people’s patience. I admire their sacrifices: taking on mortgages at any cost—many buy early for residency purposes, and homes in desirable school catchments cost at least [amount removed], an astronomical figure. They endure the pressure of a big city and suppress their own desires. They sacrifice everything for their children, from competing alongside them from early education onward to asking both sets of grandparents to help with childcare. And all this sacrifice is simply to keep the children from being forced by educational selection onto a factory production line, to give them some choice over their future lives.

Seeing their numb smiles and their efforts to appear at peace with it suddenly frightened me. It was like that familiar old story about the shepherd boy: what is the meaning of life repeating itself over and over? If living is only about letting the next generation repeat the same life, surely that departs from something fundamental about being human. I still believe people should live for themselves. If a big city is only the starting point of one cycle after another, its outwardly glamorous life may not be so enviable. I keep wondering how long it has been since we saw days full of sunshine: laughing in the sun, without the exhaustion of leaving early and returning late, those carefree summer days. Perhaps the pressures of society are too great, forcing people to yield and place their hopes in the next generation, hoping their children can live fully as themselves. I wonder whether those still working away from home in big cities, telling their elders only the good news, have really lived the wonderful lives their parents hoped for. And will their own hopes for their children help those children escape another cycle? The steep fall in birth rates in recent years may offer an answer, while the problems it brings will be a bitter fruit our generation must taste. I feel rather pessimistic when I remember visiting [place removed] and seeing grey-haired people struggling to earn a living on the front line of service work.

I try to keep myself as busy as possible to escape these seemingly fixed patterns of life. Perhaps concentrating on exam preparation for now is the only way to stop overthinking. I have rambled on a great deal; I hope you do not mind. It is late at night. May everyone who feels lost have a good dream.

Thank you for taking the time to read my email. Finally, I wish you all the best!

Warm regards,

Qingyu

NO. 19Reply

Airing to Qingyu

25 Aug 2023

Qingyu,

Since our last exchange, quite a lot has happened at work for me too. Even my monthly publication stopped for a quarter. Things have now settled and improved, though, so I can tell you about that experience.

[Timing of a role change and business area removed.] The workload was very heavy, I was not learning, and my manager was also struggling to keep up and withstand the pressure. After [length of time removed], I said I wanted to leave. That was only the immediate trigger. Looking back over [period of employment removed], the work had been much the same: swept along by business demands, feeling as though I was standing still. Every day I was like Sisyphus, pushing a product-shaped stone from the bottom of the mountain to the top, somewhat meaninglessly, then pushing the next one. It never ended, and I could not see the point. Yes, people say there is something to learn from any project, but surely you need time and energy to reflect as well. Pushing without pause, round after round, sometimes overloaded and pushing several stones at once, is hard to sustain.

After telling my manager I wanted to leave, I prepared for interviews and received a good offer. But when I spoke to the director, I was persuaded to stay. After [details of negotiations and terms removed], I am now in a very comfortable position, learning across roles. I feel that only in the past month have I found the best working state for me, and I am finally no longer standing still.

Returning to your question, I personally agree very much with your view. I do not have a strong wish to have children either, and I do not want to live a life that is too exhausting or lacks a sense of meaning—which is why I have been seeking change over these past few months. Each of us is here to live our own life first; only then can we consider other things. With society under so much pressure, simply living out a peaceful life is already difficult, and it is hard to consider much else. If social expectations push us into raising the next generation regardless of our financial circumstances, both we and the children suffer. There is nothing a person absolutely has to do. The moment you stop caring about others’ opinions and begin doing what you want is the moment of “freedom.”

I hope both you and I can be free.

[The original letter included a film or television screenshot. Its subtitles read: “The minute I stopped caring what other people thought and started doing what I wanted to do, is the minute I finally felt… Free!” This is quoted dialogue from the screenshot, not the author’s original wording.]

Good luck with your exam preparation,

Airing
