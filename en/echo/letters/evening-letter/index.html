Weiguang to Airing

9 Oct 2025

Hi Aring,

Happy Mid-Autumn Festival and National Day holiday.

I am writing from [city removed], where the temperature has suddenly dropped. The weather has pulled us forcibly from one season into another, as if pointing out how far the progress bar of time has moved.

Although we have added each other on WeChat, I still seem to prefer a form of correspondence that asks for no immediate response. I can sit alone, offline in spirit, and finish this aimless ramble without forcing it into a logical shape.

I recently changed jobs and am continuing the repetitive life of a programmer at [company removed]. I have just completed my probation review too. I do not know whether it has something to do with AI’s development, but lately the speed of time feels unreal. It holds more events, yet they pass in a briefer thought. That feels like a paradox.

“It’s been a long time since I walked the road to school; I’ve recently changed jobs too.” That is the song I played over and over at [former workplace removed] when I wanted to change jobs. Now I can occasionally bear to listen to its opening again. A friend recently said songs absorb the feelings and memories of the time when we hear them, but listening repeatedly later mixes in part of our next self. So he saves songs full of memories for rare, cherished listens. I realized I am not alone in this. The songs I repeat and the songs I write are time capsules, keeping part of who I was. In October in [city removed], hearing the first two lines of the song “魚仔”, I can still feel that summer’s fierce sunshine in [city removed], and the cool breeze when I finally reached the space beneath the footbridge.

Only recently have I really felt that time is finite. I need to choose, to subtract things from life, yet everything seems hard to give up. I bought some printed books, including one in traditional Chinese with vertical text. I have to read certain sentences over and over to assemble their meaning; I cannot let my attention drift. Each time I lose the thread and go back, I feel inefficient and wonder why I cannot simply waste time in this leisurely way. The older we get, the more we seem to need to attach some reasonable meaning to everything before we can feel entitled to do it.

At this point, this ramble seems rather too scattered, and I am hesitating to send it. But email is a form without much pressure. I can set this lightly constrained message in a bottle afloat. This year I still have not truly become lighter. In many ways I am still struggling with myself. But at least I no longer resist my circumstances so fiercely.

Finally, I wish you time to clear your mind, and peace.

Weiguang

Airing to Weiguang

24 Oct 2025

Hey Weiguang,

Happy October 24, Programmers’ Day.

I now work in [work location removed], so I no longer have the Mid-Autumn and National Day holidays, but I hope you enjoyed them.

When we last spoke, I was still preparing for interviews. Now, like you, I have just passed probation at [company removed]. After receiving your letter early this month, I also traveled to [business-trip city removed] for work. By coincidence, the weather warmed up on the two days I was there.

Our experiences seem similar, but one difference is that I seem to have lost that sense of time’s reality. Your letter made me suddenly realize that 2025 was already nearing its end. Life here is even more monotonous than in [previous city removed]: no four seasons, life within a finely made little space. Work pressure consumes my energy, leaving no attention for anything else. Time is completely full. Perhaps that is why I have lost my sensitivity to daily life and my sense of time.

Only today, when I could no longer keep going and took a day off, did I slow down enough to read your words carefully. Ironically, even on my day off there were [count removed] company calls, [count removed] work messages and [count removed] messages tagging me. Only today, when I could no longer keep going and took a day off, did I slow down enough to read your words carefully.

I did not keep reading those messages. I simply went downstairs for a leisurely meal and watched a show at home. Going out to eat, I discovered that my favorite [details of local food shops removed] had already closed, so I reluctantly ate something else. Then I went for a haircut and found the staff at the little shop downstairs had changed. [Details connecting public posts and third-party identities have been removed.]

Everything around me had been changing quietly; I simply had not noticed. [Specific work responsibilities removed.] My sensitivity to performance data was growing. But my sensitivity to daily life was gradually fading.

Fortunately, I stopped. Today I had to pick up that set of scales again. Compared with you, I am another extreme in the opposite direction. Only when I stopped did I have time to reflect on memories of the past.

I hope I can find my rhythm again, and that you can reconcile your own sense of value. Time is the same for everyone; I would not call it wasted. To me, if I can look at a sunset and let my mind wander, that is a way of using time too. So do not keep searching for some prescribed meaning. Go ahead with conviction.

Airing
