Hello Airing. I hope you will forgive this unsolicited letter!

I am writing mainly to ask you about planning a life.

First, a brief introduction: I am graduating this year, 〔combination of universities, qualifications and subject withheld〕. I have signed an offer for a backend development role at 〔company withheld〕, 〔specific starting date withheld〕.

Here is how I came across you. During 〔specific stage of study withheld〕, I was interning with 〔internship team withheld〕. The workload was not too heavy, and I would browse the internal knowledge platform in my spare time. Once I found you, I immediately followed you on every platform.

Airing, my first question is how to live with composure. Today I saw an idea that there are only two kinds of life: one in which you first work out what kind of person you want to become, then earn money; and another in which earning money is all you think about. These lead to different outcomes: being the master of money, or being carried along by it. I desperately want to be the first kind of person, but know that the second, a life accepted under pressure, is much more common.

I came from 〔region where I grew up withheld〕. Let me offer a superficial description of “people like me”: most are sensitive, self-conscious, and afraid to step onto the stage. We admire other people's brilliance while hesitating over whether to perform ourselves. By the time the stage lights go out, our palms are drenched in sweat, yet we have never risen from our seats. Such people are lonely too. From primary school to secondary school to university, one wave of people after another arrives and leaves. We do not dare—or, more often, do not have the energy—to sustain another friendship. Simply moving forward has already taken too much.

I have kept running, breathless on every leg of the journey, trying hard to keep up a respectable appearance. But as my student years approach their end, I am starting to panic. I do not have a strong inner foundation that gives me the courage to face the uncertainty ahead calmly. I know this question may have no answer, but have asked it anyway, perhaps inappropriately. Sorry!

My second subject is career planning. AI has developed so quickly over the past two years, with new technologies and products arriving one after another. I am now full of anxiety about backend development, worried that the next tipping point will destroy my own prospects. How can I keep earning a living in this era? 🥣 The view from a mountaintop differs from the view at its foot. I know my present abilities and perspective are not enough to see through this fog, and hope you can point me in a direction.

My thoughts are scattered and the logic confused. Please forgive me!

〔Shutong〕

NO. 13Reply

Airing to Shutong

21 Mar 2026

〔Shutong〕,

It is good to hear from you. I am writing this reply high above the ground, on a flight.

I can feel uncertainty and worry in every word, followed by your conclusion that the cause lies deep within yourself. Reading that brings a twinge of sadness.

I will try to respond to your two questions from my own perspective.

The first question:

It is true that we cannot change the environment or what has already happened, but we can change how we understand things and our experiences. Everything has two sides. Past experiences may make you more prone to feeling inferior, but they have also cultivated sensitivity to your surroundings. You are more attentive to your own feelings and to other people's. I think that is a valuable quality. Hold on to it and add a little confidence, and you can become gentler and more modest than most. Only when the layers of defense imprisoning the real self are peeled away and dismantled does personal growth truly begin. From that foundation of kindness, you can see more deeply and further ahead. As confusion falls away, courage will grow naturally.

All worry about uncertainty comes from not seeing far enough ahead.

The second question:

I suggest following more developments and tools at the forefront of AI, trying them, and integrating them into your life and work. Also make use of AI now to develop yourself—not only in AI, but in technology more broadly—to consolidate your understanding and cultivate judgment.

I expect the boundaries between development roles to begin breaking down over the next two years. That would be unkind to junior programmers, so you also need to prepare by deliberately building your ability to transfer what you know. For example, after work you could use vibe coding to try things in other fields, and use AI to study knowledge across disciplines systematically. This matters.

I wish you well at work!

Airing
